## Goal

When this is done, a persona keeps its own space and never starts a new effort by itself. What its self-review finds leaves it as a record to the coordinator persona and never becomes a goal in its own tree, so a finding is kept and the worker's queued work keeps the active slot. A long-term goal is held in the persona's own store, is shown in the goal status, and can never be the active work. A persona with no open work and a long-term goal is asked once a day for the next piece of work toward it, and the proposal takes the same road as a finding. The tools that start a new effort refuse in any turn the operator or the coordinator persona did not start. Where the operator rules for Section 6, a top goal the worker planned itself, whose work is all done, completes without a model being asked what comes next. The coordinator persona tells the operator about each finding and proposal in one line and passes it to the architect, who writes the plan, brings the operator a decision ask, or closes it with a reason. It matters because on 2026-09-21 the plugin's self-review wrote a goal into the dev-plugin persona's tree asking for work on the persona plugin, which that persona's repository does not hold. The node activated itself, the idle nudge quoted it twice within two minutes, and the only thing that kept the worker on its plan was the worker's own judgment. The same day the planner, asked whether the dev-discord persona's finished top goal was complete, answered with two plans for work already done, and the plugin activated one.

## Intent

**The frame, in the operator's words.** A persona is "an individual developer on the team, I trust them to maintain their space and pick their work, and get it done, and suggest new things they found." Making smaller goals for itself, selecting, reordering, parking, pivoting, adjusting settings and researching are the persona's own. "When it comes to where the product is going, bigger visions, larger efforts, that's why we have the architect and the steward, it's like a senior developer/designer and product owner/project manager." They weigh what is needed and prepare "that work for the developers to run as they have capacity." On the self-review: "I think it's a great mechanism. I don't necessarily need to approve it, but I would like to at least get a message or know about items like that, in case I disagree with them strongly." A long-term goal is "the idea of what we're working towards."

**What done needs.** Nothing a persona finds is thrown away, and none of it takes the active slot from queued work. The operator hears about each finding and each proposal once, in one line, and approves nothing to let it be captured. All proposed work, from a finding or toward a long-term goal, travels one road: the coordinator persona, then the architect. The line between a persona's own work and a proposal is whether the work needs a new plan document. The plugin enforces the half of that line it can see.

**What done does not need.** A persona judging the size of its own idea through a model call. A second kaizen inbox. A new approval step for the operator. A long-term goal shared by the whole fleet. A coded wake of the architect. Knowledge in the plugin of which persona can act on which work.

**Alternatives refused.**
- The finding stays in the finder's tree as a parked node: two of the three workers can never do plugin work, so it waits forever.
- The plugin appends the finding to the backlog file of the plugin's repository: a persona would write in a repository it does not hold.
- The claude-kit kaizen inbox as the carrier: that inbox is a notes file in the kit's clone, written by a session's commit, and a note not about the kit is routed out of it.
- The dev persona keeps its own findings as a shortcut: it is the one case that would need the plugin to know who can act on what.
- A persona plans for itself under a long-term goal: a worker on a cheaper model decides what gets built with no plan document behind it.
- A long-term goal as context only: it never produces work.
- A size threshold in hours or lines for the autonomy line: the plugin cannot measure either before the work exists.
- A classifier call on each new goal: it puts the judgment on the cheaper model, which is the failure of 2026-09-21.
- A file under the persona's folder, or a roster field, for the long-term goal: a second record beside the store, or a keeper restart on every edit.

**Rulings, all the operator's, all 2026-09-21.** 1: the operator writes long-term goals, or a persona proposes one and it is adopted on his yes. 2: a persona proposes when it has no work open. 3: a long-term goal lives in the persona's own store and is written through a plugin tool. 4: a self-review finding is kept, the operator gets a message and gives no approval, and it never takes the active slot. 5: a finding goes to the coordinator persona as a record, which tells the operator in one line and wakes the architect, who plans it, asks, or closes it with a reason. 6: work toward an adopted long-term goal is proposed on that same road. 7: the autonomy line is whether the work needs a new plan document. 8, made after the plan was written, on Section 6: a top goal whose plans are all finished is complete, and nothing is asked to judge it. In his words, "If you finished everything in a plan, then the top goal should be complete." He added that a plan left only with a check someone runs after release is finished too: "That's a handoff, a 'someone needs to go check this later' kind of thing." The worker completes such a plan and hands the check off. It never holds the plan open for it. He also raised a faster model as the judge of whether a plan is done, and closed on the reading this plan takes: where every part is complete the goal is complete, and no evaluation is needed.

## Approach

**Vocabulary.** The coordinator persona is the persona named by the plugin's `coordinatorPersona` setting (`hooks/index.ts:1899-1902`), which this fleet calls the steward. The architect is the persona named by `ARCHITECT_PERSONA` in `bin/supervise.sh`. The finder is the persona whose self-review produced a finding. A record is an inbox record in the commons store (`InboxRecord`, `hooks/operator.ts:24-39`).

**One road.** A finding and a proposal are both ordinary inbox records of kind `say` addressed to the coordinator persona. A finding's text opens with `[FINDING]` and a proposal's with `[PROPOSAL]`. The leads are closed at those two, and a lead counts only as the first characters of the record's text: a `[PROPOSAL]` quoted further down a message does not make it one, the `[KAIZEN]` thread message is not a record at all, and a proposal turn answered `No proposal.` sends nothing. No new record kind is added, because the delivery code keys on nothing in the text and the coordinator persona's standing instruction can key on the lead. A persona may also send a `[PROPOSAL]` by its own `agentic_say` call at any time, which is the road the autonomy line sends it down. The reach rule already admits a named persona's owner session writing to the coordinator persona (`hooks/operator.ts:643`), so it does not change.

**The plugin-side writer.** Today `hooks/index.ts:6484` is the only `writeInboxRecord` call, inside the `agentic_say` handler. Section 1 adds one exported helper in `hooks/operator.ts`, `sendPluginRecord(store, target, writerSessionId, text)`, which takes the next sequence number with `getHighestInboxSeq` and writes with `writeInboxRecord`, and returns the writer session id and the sequence number. Section 1 and Section 5 both call it. The `agentic_say` handler keeps its own path, since it carries `answers` and `urgent` and its own checks.

**Why the record is not the ledger.** An inbox record does not last. A `pending` record whose writer holds no live claim at delivery is set `skipped` (`hooks/index.ts:3686-3700`), and a persona restart gives its session a new id. A delivered record is swept 24 hours after its last change (`hooks/operator.ts:363-384`). So the finder keeps its own ledger in its state file, and the record is only the carrier. The ledger is `monitor.selfReview.sent`, a list of entries `{ signal, text, sentAt, writer, seq, delivered }`. The settle step runs on every controller tick of an owner session, ahead of the self-review block's eligibility check, and not only on a tick where a review is due: a persona with little to do takes few turns, and a review counted in turns could leave a lost record unnoticed for days. It reads back the record of every entry with `delivered` false, with `readInboxRecord`. A record whose status is `delivered`, `answered` or `resolved` sets `delivered` true. So does an absent record, because a `pending` record is never swept (`hooks/operator.ts:366`) and an absent one has therefore already left `pending`. A record that reads `skipped` is sent again under the current session with the same text, and the entry's `writer` and `seq` are replaced while `sentAt` is kept. A `pending` record younger than `FINDING_UNROUTABLE_AFTER_MS`, 24 hours from `sentAt`, is left alone. One still `pending` past that has no coordinator to take it, and the entry takes the unroutable path below: it is announced on the finder's own thread and marked delivered, and the record is left in the store. A record that was skipped and then rolled out of the store before the finder next ticked reads absent and is not sent again, which the plan accepts, since it needs the finder down for longer than the record survives. An entry that is delivered and older than the cool-off is dropped at the settle step, so the list stays short, unless it is its signal's latest entry, since the next finding for that signal counts only events after that entry's `sentAt`. The proposal ledger in Section 5 settles the same way. Durability past delivery is the coordinator persona's and the architect's, stated in their standing instructions.

**The cool-off.** A signal that was sent is not raised again for `FINDING_COOLOFF_MS`, seven days, counted from `sentAt`, and only events after `sentAt` count toward the next finding. This replaces today's rule, which suppresses a signal while a goal node carrying it is open and counts events since the last such node closed (`hooks/self-review.ts:325-329`). The finder cannot see when a finding is acted on, since the record may be swept first, so a fixed window is the rule that needs no answer to come back. The constant is exported beside the three `KAIZEN_` constants and is not a setting.

**The self-review's own configuration change stays.** The `long_turns` finding can halve `selfReviewEveryTurns` (`hooks/self-review.ts:332-338`), applied at `hooks/index.ts:3934-3945`. That branch keeps applying the change and logging `kaizen_config_adjusted`. Its rationale line now rides in a `[FINDING]` record instead of the `[KAIZEN]` thread message.

**Where there is no coordinator to reach.** A session whose persona is `default`, or whose write `mayReachPersona` refuses, has no road. A named persona on an install where no session ever holds the coordinator persona has no road either, though the write succeeds: `coordinatorPersona` always resolves to a name and the reach rule admits the write whether or not anything holds that name, which is the case the settle step's 24-hour rule catches. On this path the finder logs `finding_unroutable`, writes no goal node, and announces the finding on its own thread through the `[KAIZEN]` frame. The frame's first line is unchanged. Each line under it is the finding's rationale and its signal, with no node id, since there is no node. Three of the four rationale texts in `describe` say today that a kaizen goal is being raised, which would now be untrue, so Section 1 rewords that clause in each of the three to say what the finding asks for, and no rationale says a goal or a node was raised. The fourth, `long_turns`, raises no goal and carries only its cadence change. An already-written node routed on this path is still set `abandoned`, and its note says the finding was announced on the persona's own thread. The ledger entry is written with `delivered` true, an empty `writer` and a `seq` of 0, so it is never read back or retried, and it starts the cool-off like any other. This keeps a single-session install from losing findings silently. In the coordinator persona's own session the write goes to its own inbox, which `writeInboxRecord` permits, and the record is delivered to it like any other. That delivery carries the `COORDINATOR` ground, because the writer holds the coordinator persona (`hooks/operator.ts:642`), so Section 4's gate has to keep it out: a delivery whose record text opens with `[FINDING]` or `[PROPOSAL]` never counts as the coordinator persona's turn.

**Goal nodes the self-review already wrote.** Stores on this machine hold them: the dev-plugin persona's store holds two open ones on 2026-09-21. On the first tick after the upgrade, every node with a `kaizenSignal` and a status of `pending`, `active`, `paused` or `blocked` is sent as a finding, entered in the ledger, set `abandoned` with a note naming the record, and logged as `kaizen_node_routed`. Its text is `[FINDING] <persona> <signal>`, a newline, then the node's `objective`, which already carries the count. This routing runs with the settle step, on every tick of an owner session and ahead of the self-review block, so an upgraded persona's nodes are routed at once and the routed signal is already in the ledger when a review next reads it. Where the node was the active one, `activeGoalId` is cleared and `activateNext` runs. The `kaizenSignal` field stays on the `GoalNode` type as an optional field for those stores. `kaizenSortKey` has no caller left and is deleted with its unit cases.

**The long-term goal is a list beside the tree, not a node in it.** Ruling 3 fixes where it lives and how it is written: the persona's own store, through a tool. The sketch the operator approved called it a new kind of node. Reading the tree code changed the mechanism and nothing the operator sees. A node in the tree would have to be skipped by `activateNext`, `isActivationEligible` and `completeLeaf`'s upward walk, where an unfinishable child would stop its root from ever completing (`hooks/agent-state.ts:611-745`). It would be dropped when `goal_create` replaces the tree (`hooks/index.ts:5919`). And a parentless one would break the many `goals.find((g) => g.parentId === null)` reads that find the root. So `AgentState` gains `longTermGoals: LongTermGoal[]`, with `LongTermGoal` as `{ id, title, objective, createdAt }`. `parseState` fills an absent field with an empty list, the way it fills `monitor.env`, and the state version stays 4. No tree walker reads the list, so it can never be the active work by construction.

**The tool.** `goal_longterm` registers beside the other goal tools, inside the same `arming !== "reader"` gate (`hooks/index.ts:2112`). It takes `action`, closed at `add` and `drop`. `add` takes `title` and `objective` and returns the new id. `drop` takes `id` and `reason`, removes the entry and logs the reason. An edit is a drop and an add. A persona that wants a long-term goal of its own proposes it as a `[PROPOSAL]` record like any other work. On the operator's yes, the operator says so on that persona's thread, and the persona calls `goal_longterm` in that turn, which is one the operator started. The list is capped at five entries and an `add` past the cap is denied, since the list is injected into a prompt in Section 5. `goal_status` prints the list under the tree, one line each, under a `Long-term goals:` heading, and prints the heading with `(none)` where the list is empty and a tree exists.

**The autonomy line, the half the plugin sees.** The acts that start a new effort are closed at four: `goal_create`, `goal_add` where the resolved kind is `plan`, `goal_longterm` with `add`, and `goal_longterm` with `drop`. Each is allowed only in a turn one of two parties started. The operator's turns are closed at the prompt origin kinds `composer`, `bridge`, `channel` and `sdk` as the harness stamps them on `prompt.submit` (`PromptOrigin`, `.claude/types/claude-code.d.ts:4336`). `sdk` is on the list because the supervisor writes the operator's launch prompt to the child's stdin (`bin/supervise.sh:2856-2875`). The coordinator persona's turns are the delivery turns whose record was delivered under the `COORDINATOR` ground, whether the record is a `say` or an `answer`. Every other turn refuses: a turn the plugin opened with kind `nudge` or `plugin`, a delivery under any other ground, and the origin kinds `task-notification`, `scheduled-trigger`, `peer`, `peer-send-message`, `projects-relay`, `coordinator`, `observer`, `observer-activity`, `auto-continuation` and `unclassified`. The refusal text tells the persona to send the idea to the coordinator persona with `agentic_say`, opening with `[PROPOSAL]`. `goal_add` with kind `task`, `goal_done`, `goal_edit`, `goal_resume` and `goal_status` are never gated, since they work inside what the persona already holds. The planner's own plan nodes (`hooks/index.ts:4350-4409`) are not a tool call and are not gated, since they break down a root the operator wrote.

The turn start handler already captures `currentTurnIsChannelOrigin` from the hook's `e.origin.kind` (`hooks/index.ts:6821`, `5059`) and `currentTurnKind` from the matched expected turn (`5095`). Section 4 captures the whole origin kind the same way, and carries the delivery's ground on both expected-turn entries a delivery can build, the answer entry near `hooks/index.ts:3668` and the drain entry near `3752`, so turn start can capture it too. Where a turn matched an expected turn, that entry's kind and ground decide and the captured origin is ignored, since the origin flag is a one-step handoff that a plugin submit never resets. A turn whose prompt opens with `[SUPERVISOR-PRIMING]` is refused whatever its origin kind: the supervisor writes it to the child's stdin on every launch, including the relaunch after a root completes, and neither the operator nor the coordinator persona started it. The hook already reads that marker (`hooks/index.ts:6819`). A record that breaks into a running turn does not change that turn's origin: the gate reads what opened the turn.

**The idle proposal.** The tick's no-active-leaf branch (`hooks/index.ts:4425-4434`) returns when `activateNext` finds nothing. Section 5 adds one step there. It runs when all of these hold: the session is the owner, its persona is not the coordinator persona, `longTermGoals` is not empty, `hasStartableWork` from `hooks/agent-state.ts` is false (the architect's amendment of 2026-09-22: no node is `active` and no `pending` node is activation-eligible, so an all-paused or all-blocked tree counts as idle), no turn is open, and `PROPOSAL_EVERY_MS`, 24 hours, has passed since `monitor.proposal.askedAt`. It stamps `askedAt` before the submit, for the reason the nudge floor is spent first (`hooks/index.ts:4889-4900`), and submits a `[PROPOSE]` plugin turn. The frame lists the long-term goals and asks for the single next piece of work toward one of them: what it is, why now, and the repository it belongs in. It tells the persona to send that with `agentic_say` to the coordinator persona opening with `[PROPOSAL]`, to start none of it, and to answer `No proposal.` and send nothing where it has none worth making. ## Sections of Work

### 1. A finding leaves the finder
