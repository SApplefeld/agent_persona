## Goal

When this ships, and the operator names `memory-kind` in a persona's `jevLive` roster field, that persona's turn-end memory step asks Jev first and Haiku only on what Jev leaves open. Where Jev is confident the exchange holds nothing memorable, the step makes neither of the two Haiku calls it makes today and records that it skipped them. Where Jev is unsure, or fails, or times out, the step runs exactly as it does today. One journaled call in five keeps running Haiku whatever Jev said, so the rate at which the gate would have skipped a memory Haiku kept stays measurable after promotion, from the same journal that measured it before. The confidence floor is a roster field, `memoryGateDiscardPercent`, default 90, so the operator tunes it per persona in `fleet.json` and never edits a script. With `memory-kind` absent from `jevLive`, nothing changes: the site keeps asking Haiku and shadowing Jev as it does today.

## Intent

The frame, in the operator's words on 2026-09-25: Jev could decide "IF there are memories BEFORE passing to Haiku to evaluate them", which "would be far faster and cheaper", with the question narrowed and constrained.

What the journal already says. The `memory-kind` site has shadowed Jev beside Haiku on every non-nudged turn since 2026-09-20. Over 3,337 answered calls across every persona on this machine, Haiku said `discard` on 3,098 and kept 239. Read as a gate on Jev's `discard` probability: at a floor of 0.9, Jev would have skipped 1,290 of the 3,098 discards (42%) and 5 of the 239 keeps (2%); at 0.8, 71% and 16%; at 0.95, 15% and under 1%. The five keeps lost at 0.9 are, on a hand read, a task-notification report labelled `fact` three times and a steward reconciliation summary twice, none a memory worth holding. So the gate costs nothing the store wants, and the saving is a Haiku classify on roughly four turns in ten.

What done needs to do: put Jev ahead of Haiku at the memory site when the question is live; skip both Haiku calls on a confident `discard`; fall back to today's path on every failure; keep one call in five measurable; make the floor a roster field; state the amended behavior in the documents. What done does not need to do: no new question, since `memory-kind` is the question and it is already shadowed; no change to what Haiku distills or how the entry is stored; no reading of the journal by the plugin, since the promotion bar is judged offline; no change to the four other shadow sites.

Alternatives refused:
- Jev replaces the Haiku classify outright, with Haiku writing the text on every non-discard Jev answer. Refused: Jev's highest-probability label disagrees with Haiku's keep on two keeps in three, so the store would shrink to a third of its intake on an unmeasured basis; the probability floor keeps Haiku's judgment on every unsure turn.
- A separate audit sample instead of the journal's holdout split. Refused: the journal already marks one stamp in five `holdout` by a hash of the id, and the existing calibration scripts read that split; a second sampling rule would be a second thing to calibrate.
- Passing the persona's existing memories in the state so Jev can answer "nothing new". Refused here: that is a different question with its own instructions and its own shadow period, and it is named under Out of Scope as the follow-on.
- A fixed floor constant. Refused: the operator tunes fleet behavior through the roster, and the right floor moves as the journal grows.

Rulings: none at the write.

## Approach

**The gate is one branch at the memory site, taken only where `memory-kind` is live.** The site (`hooks/index.ts` near 7635, the block opening "Memory curation: distill, don't snapshot") today binds the state text, asks Haiku, shadows Jev with Haiku's answer, and on a non-`discard` answer asks Haiku to distill. The site tests the branch itself before any call: `memory-kind` is in `jevLive` and `jevMode` is `shadow`. Where either fails, the site is byte-for-byte today's, Haiku then `shadowAsk` with Haiku's value, and `liveAsk` is never called, so no shadow line is journaled with a null Haiku value. Where both hold, the site awaits `liveAsk` on the same state. `liveAsk` returns the stamp id of the call it journaled with either the validated answer or the closed failure reason. On a failure the site runs today's Haiku classify and distill, makes no shadow call (the live call already journaled its failure), and logs one `memory_gate_fallback` decision naming the reason and the stamp id. On an answer, the site reads `probabilities.discard` and the stamp's split through `splitOf`. Where the split is `dev` and `Math.round(probability * 100)` is at or above `memoryGateDiscardPercent`, it makes no Haiku call, stores nothing, and logs one `memory_gate_skipped` decision carrying the probability, the stamp id and the split. Otherwise it runs today's Haiku classify and distill, makes no shadow call, logs one `memory_gate_passed` decision carrying the stamp id, the split and the one condition that let it through, `holdout` where the split is holdout whatever the probability, else `below-floor`, and writes one outcome line of kind `haiku_kind` against the live call's stamp id whose `value` field carries Haiku's label, so the journal holds Jev's answer and Haiku's for the same input on every passed call, exactly as a shadow answer line does.

**`liveAsk` gains the stamp id and the failure reason in its return.** The goal-every-turn plan defines `liveAsk` as returning the validated `ChoiceAnswer` or null, with null on the not-live path and on every failure alike. This plan widens it to return null only where the question is not live (the path on which it calls `shadowAsk` itself), and otherwise `{ stampId, answer }` on a validated answer or `{ stampId, reason }` on a closed `SeamFailureReason`, because the gate needs the stamp for the split and the outcome line and needs to name why it fell back. That plan's two call sites, the `turn-open` ask in the `prompt.submit` hook where a record is opened (its section 4) and the `turn-disposition` ask at the true turn boundary where a record is closed (its section 5), read `.answer` and treat a `reason` object as they treat null today. `memory-kind` joins `PROMOTABLE_SET_IDS`, and `haiku_kind` joins `OUTCOME_KINDS` in `hooks/decision-journal.ts`.

**The floor is a roster field, on the path every numeric setting takes.** `memoryGateDiscardPercent` joins the keeper's `$map` in `Build-SupervisorInvocation` (`bin/keeper-functions.ps1`), so a roster entry carrying it launches the supervisor with it in the environment. `bin/supervise.sh` reads `MEMORY_GATE_DISCARD_PERCENT="${memoryGateDiscardPercent:-90}"` beside the other settings, checks it with `positive_number` and then refuses a value outside 50 to 100 with an `ERROR:` line naming the setting, and `emit_settings_json` in `bin/agentic-common.sh` writes `"memoryGateDiscardPercent":<n>` under both plugin ids, with the variable added to the digits-only shape loop at line 153. The plugin declares `memoryGateDiscardPercent` in `.claude-plugin/plugin.json`'s `userConfig` beside `heartbeatMs` (line 11), type number, default 90, since that manifest declares every key the plugin reads from its options and whether an undeclared key arrives is not established. It reads `cfg.memoryGateDiscardPercent` at registration where it reads `heartbeatMs` (`hooks/index.ts` near 2849): an absent or non-numeric value is the silent default of 90, as `heartbeatMs` falls to its own; a present number is rounded to an integer, and one outside 50 to 100 is clamped to 90 with one `setting_clamped` decision. A percent rather than a decimal, because every supervisor setting is a whole number and `positive_number` is the one rule they take.

**The promotion is the operator's roster edit, on the bar the journal already met.** Nothing in this plan turns the gate on. The operator names `memory-kind` in a persona's `jevLive` field when ready, one persona at a time if wanted. The bar the goal-every-turn plan sets for its own questions is a labeling pass over the shadow journal; for this question the pass is the count and the hand read recorded under Intent, and the holdout outcome lines keep it readable afterwards.

**The surfaces that speak this contract**, from a read of the tree at `fd5b2d6`: `hooks/index.ts` (the memory site near 7635, `shadowAsk` at 218, the `heartbeatMs` read at 2849), `hooks/question-catalog.ts` (`MEMORY_KIND` at 75, `PROMOTABLE_SET_IDS` once the goal-every-turn plan adds it), `hooks/decision-journal.ts` (`splitOf` at 189, `OUTCOME_KINDS` at 120), `bin/supervise.sh` (the settings block near 343, the startup checks), `bin/agentic-common.sh` (`emit_settings_json`, the shape loop at 153), `bin/keeper-functions.ps1` (`$map`), `.claude-plugin/plugin.json` (`userConfig`), `.kit/controller-tick-test.mjs` (the seam invariance instrument near 20866), `.kit/tick-harness.mjs` (the Jev fake at 145 and the `jevChoiceResponse` helper at 561), `.kit/decision-journal-unit-test.mjs`, `.kit/supervisor-model-test.sh`, `.kit/settings-plugin-key-test.sh`, `.kit/keeper-unit-test.mjs`, `README.md` (the Decision seam table at 757, the `memory-kind` state paragraph at 997, the Limitations latency line at 730, the Startup Checks paragraph at 310, the outcome `kind` row at 956 and the paragraph at 962) and `docs/architecture.md` (the symptom table whose rows open with what an operator sees and name where to look, near 295).
