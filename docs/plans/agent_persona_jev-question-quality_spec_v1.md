## Goal

When this plan is done, each of the plugin's weak shadow questions has a gold-labelled sample graded by two independent Opus labellers from a written rubric, a bar stated in this document and in the README, and either a re-posed question shipped as a new version in the catalog or a recorded retirement. The controller question is posed on the decision the controller makes, the turn-score question carries the text that decides it, and the plan-health request asks only what something reads. A repeatable tool draws the sample, runs the labellers, and scores any question version against gold, against Haiku and against outcomes, so the bar can be read again on fresh turns after this plan merges. Nothing goes live: no question joins `jevLive`, the per-persona settings list of question ids whose Jev answer a branch may read, which the goal-every-turn plan ships.

## Approach

**A gold label is the ground truth, and every figure in this plan is read against it first.** The journal's agreement column compares Jev with Haiku, and the experiment showed the incumbent is wrong often enough that agreement cannot rank a rewording. So section 1 builds the sample and the labels before section 3 changes a word, and section 2 records the baseline accuracy of every v1 question against gold in its Chapter. A re-posed question is then scored the same way on the same sample by replay, and again on fresh turns after merge. The journal alone cannot supply a v2 state, since a v1 call line never carried the worker's last answer, carries the scorer's answer to 1,000 characters only, and predates the tool summary. So the sampler joins every call line to the turn that produced it in the persona's transcript under `~/.claude/projects/<key>/<sessionId>.jsonl`, by session id, taking the most recent turn in that transcript whose last line precedes the call's timestamp, since the controller fires between turns and the scorer and the plan-health request fire after a turn's last line, and builds the v2 state fields from the transcript: the opening prompt, the worker's final message, and a tool-activity summary computed from the turn's tool-use blocks in the shape the goal-every-turn plan defines. A call whose transcript turn is gone, the harness keeps about thirty days, is dropped from the sample and counted in the Chapter. The replay sends the v1 state under the v1 wording and the v2-shaped state under the v2 wording, so the two figures are read on the same turns.

**The five lessons are the recipe for every re-posed question.** The state carries the text that decides the question: for the controller, the worker's last answer; for turn-score, the prompt that opened the turn, the answer, and the tool summary. The answer set is closed and small, and each option is the exact decision the consumer makes. Each option description carries the boundary cases the gold pass surfaced, written after the labels rather than before, so section 3 and section 4 each open by reading their question's disagreement list. A question changes version when its wording, its options or its state shape change, and the journal separates versions by the label.

**A question survives only with a consumer and a bar.** The controller consumes turn-score's scores and its own decision. Nothing consumes the plan-health answers, which is why item 1 retires the three whose readings turn-disposition covers or whose signal is absent. A bar is a figure, a threshold, a minimum count and the split it is read on, and each surviving question's bar is stated in its section and copied to the README.

**The tools live under `.kit/jev-gold/` and read only the journal.** `sample.mjs` reads every journal file, reconstructs a null `state` from the site's previous line in the same file, joins each call to its answer, its outcome lines, and the next call at the same site and persona as hindsight, and writes a stratified sample. `label.mjs` runs the two labellers through the headless CLI with the question's rubric as the system prompt and the batch as the prompt, writes one JSON line per record per labeller, and computes kappa. `adjudicate.mjs` writes `gold.jsonl` from agreements, runs a third labeller over the disagreements, and lists the three-way splits for the main thread to read. `score.mjs` reads gold, the journal and an optional replay file, and prints per question version: accuracy, per-option precision and recall, calibration by top-probability bin, agreement with Haiku, AUC against each outcome, and one `bar:` line per bar it knows. The rubrics live beside the tools as `rubrics/<question>.md` in the shape the experiment used. Journal text never leaves the machine except to the CLI's own model and, on replay, to TypeSafe; the v1 states are the cut the operator accepted, and the wider v2 cut is asked under Assumptions.
